# Sleep and recovery for students

Muscle is built during recovery, not during the workout itself, and sleep is the
single most powerful recovery tool. Most adults need about 7–9 hours per night;
chronically short sleep blunts strength gains, increases hunger and cravings, and
makes fat loss harder by disrupting appetite hormones.

For students with irregular schedules, consistency helps more than perfection: try to
keep a roughly fixed sleep and wake time, even on weekends. Cut bright screens and
heavy caffeine in the few hours before bed, since caffeine can linger for many hours.
A short 20-minute nap can help after a poor night, but long late-afternoon naps tend
to push back nighttime sleep.

Recovery is also about training sensibly. Hitting the same muscle hard every single
day without rest leads to fatigue and stalled progress; most muscle groups recover
well with about 48 hours between heavy sessions. If you feel persistently drained,
weak, or unmotivated, that is often a signal to sleep more and train a little less,
not to push harder.
