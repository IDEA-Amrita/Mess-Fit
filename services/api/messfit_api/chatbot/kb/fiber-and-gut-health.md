# Fibre and gut health on an Indian diet

Fibre supports digestion, steadier blood sugar, and fullness, yet many students fall
short when meals lean heavily on white rice, maida, and fried snacks. A common target
is around 25–35 g of fibre per day.

The good news is that a traditional Indian diet makes this easy when you choose well.
Whole pulses (dal, rajma, chana), whole grains (whole-wheat roti, oats, millets like
ragi and bajra), vegetables, fruit with the skin on, and a small handful of nuts or
seeds all add fibre. Swapping some white rice for millets or adding an extra vegetable
sabzi is often enough to close the gap.

Increase fibre gradually and drink enough water, since a sudden jump can cause bloating
or discomfort. If you have a gut condition such as IBS, some high-fibre foods may need
to be adjusted to your tolerance — that is individual, and worth discussing with a
doctor or dietitian rather than following generic maximums.
