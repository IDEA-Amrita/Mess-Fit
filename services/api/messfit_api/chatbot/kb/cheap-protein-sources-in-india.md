# Cheap protein sources in India

Protein is usually the hardest macro to hit on a student budget, but several
everyday Indian foods are both cheap and protein-dense. The goal for most active
students is roughly 1.6–2.0 g of protein per kg of body weight per day, spread
across meals.

Eggs are the classic value pick: around 6 g of protein each for a low cost, and easy
to boil in a hostel. Milk and curd add about 3–3.5 g per 100 ml/g and double as
calcium sources. Legumes are the backbone of vegetarian protein — dal, rajma, chana
(chickpeas), and lobia each give roughly 7–9 g per cooked katori, and pairing them
with rice or roti improves the overall amino-acid profile. Soya chunks are
exceptional value at about 50 g of protein per 100 g dry, though portion sizes are
small once rehydrated.

Paneer (about 18 g per 100 g) and roasted chana or peanuts make good top-ups between
meals. Whey protein is convenient but optional — it is just a concentrated, faster
food source, not magic. Build the base from eggs, dairy, and legumes first, then use
supplements only to fill a gap you cannot close with food.
