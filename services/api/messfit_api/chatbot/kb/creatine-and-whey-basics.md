# Creatine and whey: the basics

Two supplements have strong evidence and a good safety record for healthy adults:
creatine monohydrate and whey protein. Neither is necessary if your diet is already
solid, but both are convenient and inexpensive ways to fill gaps. This is general
guidance; anyone with a medical condition should check with a doctor first.

Creatine monohydrate helps with strength and high-intensity performance. A common
approach is about 3–5 g per day, every day, taken at any time — consistency matters
more than timing, and a "loading phase" is optional. It draws a little water into
muscle, so a small early weight bump is normal and not fat. Plain monohydrate is the
cheapest and most studied form; fancier versions rarely justify the price.

Whey protein is simply a fast, convenient protein source — useful when you cannot get
enough from meals. A typical scoop provides 20–25 g of protein. Vegetarians who avoid
whey can use a plant protein blend instead. Beyond these two, most supplements offer
little for the money; prioritise food, sleep, and training first.
