# Hydration for training in a hot climate

Hydration affects both performance and how you feel day to day. Even mild dehydration
can reduce strength, endurance, and concentration — which matters in India's heat,
where students often train in warm rooms or outdoors.

A reasonable baseline is around 30–35 ml of water per kg of body weight per day, more
when it is hot or when you train hard and sweat heavily. Rather than chugging large
amounts at once, sip steadily through the day. A practical self-check is urine colour:
pale yellow generally means well hydrated, while dark yellow suggests you need more.

For ordinary workouts under about an hour, plain water is enough; you do not need a
sports drink. For long or very sweaty sessions, replacing some electrolytes (a pinch
of salt and some carbohydrate, or nimbu-pani with salt and sugar) helps. Drink some
water before training, a few sips during, and rehydrate afterwards along with your
post-workout meal.
