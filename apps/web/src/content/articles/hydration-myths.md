Hydration advice is full of confident nonsense — magic glass counts, detox claims, and sports drinks marketed as essential. Here's what actually matters, especially when you're training in Indian heat.

## Myth: everyone needs exactly 8 glasses

The "8 glasses" rule is a rough slogan, not a law. Your needs depend on your size, activity, and the weather. A practical baseline is around **30–35 ml per kg of body weight per day**, and more when it's hot or you sweat hard. A 60 kg student is looking at roughly 2–2.5 litres baseline, higher on training days.

Better than counting glasses: **check your urine.** Pale yellow generally means well hydrated; dark yellow means drink more. Your body is the dashboard.

## Myth: you need a sports drink for every workout

For ordinary sessions under about an hour, **plain water is enough.** Sports drinks exist to replace electrolytes and carbs during *long* or *very sweaty* efforts. For a 45-minute hostel workout, they're just sugar you don't need. For a long, sweaty session, a pinch of salt and some carbs — or simple nimbu-pani with salt and sugar — does the job cheaply.

## Myth: thirst means you're already "too late"

Thirst is a normal, useful signal, not a failure. You don't need to pre-empt it obsessively. Sip steadily through the day and drink to thirst around training. The exception is intense heat or long sessions, where it's smart to drink a bit before you feel thirsty.

## Myth: more water is always better

You can overdo it. Drinking *excessive* amounts in a short time is unnecessary and, in rare extremes, harmful (it dilutes blood sodium). Steady, moderate intake beats chugging litres at once.

## Hydration and performance

Even mild dehydration measurably reduces strength, endurance, and concentration — which matters for both your workout and your exams. So hydration isn't about detox myths; it's about simply feeling and performing better.

> **Hydration myth-busting aside — hostel context:** Carry a refillable bottle to class and refill it at every break; the habit beats willpower. In hot weather, add a pinch of salt and a squeeze of lemon to water after a sweaty session instead of buying expensive sports drinks. Watch out for using sugary "energy" drinks as hydration — they're a spike, not a substitute for water.

## Quick FAQ

**Does tea/coffee dehydrate me?** Not meaningfully at normal intakes — the fluid in them still counts toward your day, even if caffeine is mildly diuretic.

**Should I drink during workouts?** A few sips are fine; for sessions under an hour you don't need to force large amounts. Rehydrate properly afterward.

**Is clear urine the goal?** Pale yellow is the target. Completely clear all day can mean you're over-drinking; deep yellow means drink more.

## Action items

1. Aim for ~30–35 ml/kg/day, more when hot or training hard.
2. Use urine colour (pale yellow) as your real hydration check.
3. Drink plain water for normal workouts; save electrolytes for long/sweaty ones.
4. Sip steadily through the day; carry a refillable bottle.
5. Skip sugary "energy" drinks as a hydration source.
