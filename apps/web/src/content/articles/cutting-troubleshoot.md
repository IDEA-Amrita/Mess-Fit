You cut out the obvious junk, you feel like you're eating less, but the weight isn't coming off. Before you slash your food to nothing, understand what's actually happening — the answer is rarely "eat even less."

## 1. The deficit isn't real yet

Just like over-eaters underestimate intake, dieters underestimate it too — the chai with sugar, the shared fries, the "small" canteen snack. These add up fast and can erase your deficit.

**Fix:** account for the extras honestly for one week. Liquid calories and fried canteen food are the usual hidden culprits.

## 2. Weekend creep

Five disciplined days and two loose weekend days can flatten your weekly average to maintenance. Fat loss is a weekly equation, not a weekday one.

**Fix:** keep weekends roughly in line. You don't need perfection — just don't undo the whole week in two days.

## 3. Water weight is masking real fat loss

This is the sneaky one. You can lose fat while the scale holds steady because of water retention — from a salty mess meal, a hard workout (muscles hold water as they repair), stress, or (for women) the menstrual cycle. The fat loss is happening; water is hiding it.

**Fix:** trust the multi-week trend and other signals — waist measurement, how clothes fit, progress photos — not a single morning's number.

## 4. You cut too hard and stalled out

Crash deficits backfire: you lose energy, move less without realising (fewer steps, less fidgeting), train worse, and sometimes lose muscle — which lowers your TDEE and stalls progress.

**Fix:** keep the deficit moderate (~300–500 kcal), protein high (1.8–2.2 g/kg), and keep lifting. A sustainable cut beats an aggressive one that you quit.

## 5. You've genuinely reached a new TDEE

A lighter body burns fewer calories, so the deficit that worked at 70 kg may be maintenance at 66 kg.

**Fix:** recalculate. Trim another ~150–200 kcal, or add some daily steps, rather than gutting your intake.

> **Hostel context:** On a cut, the mess is your friend — high-volume, filling foods like dal, sabzi, salad, and curd let you eat a satisfying plate for few calories. The danger zone is *between* meals: the canteen, the late-night Maggi, the sweet shop. Pre-decide your snacks (fruit, roasted chana, curd) so a hungry evening doesn't blow the deficit.

## Quick FAQ

**The scale jumped up overnight — did I gain fat?** Almost certainly not. Overnight gains of 0.5–1.5 kg are water and food in transit, especially after a salty or carb-heavy meal. Watch the weekly trend.

**Should I do fasted cardio / skip breakfast?** Only if it suits you. Total daily intake drives fat loss; meal timing is a minor detail.

**Will more cardio fix a stall faster?** A little helps, but protein + a moderate deficit + lifting matter more. Don't out-cardio a diet you haven't tightened.

## Action items

1. Track intake honestly for one week, including drinks and canteen extras.
2. Keep weekends consistent with weekdays.
3. Judge progress by a 2–3 week trend + waist/photos, not daily weight.
4. Hold a moderate deficit and high protein; keep lifting to protect muscle.
5. If truly stalled after 3 weeks, trim ~150–200 kcal or add steps — don't crash.
