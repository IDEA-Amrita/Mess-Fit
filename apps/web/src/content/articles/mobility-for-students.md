Between lectures, labs, and late-night study, you might sit 10+ hours a day. Your body adapts to whatever you do most — and if that's slumping in a chair, you get tight hips, a stiff upper back, cranky shoulders, and a lower back that aches for no clear reason. A few minutes of mobility a day fixes most of it.

## Why sitting causes problems

Sit long enough and certain muscles shorten and stiffen (hip flexors, chest, the back of the neck) while others switch off and weaken (glutes, deep core, upper-back muscles). The result is the classic student posture: rounded shoulders, forward head, locked-up hips. It's not damage — it's adaptation, and it reverses with movement.

## A 5–8 minute daily routine

No equipment needed. Hold each stretch ~30 seconds, do mobility drills for ~30–45 seconds:

- **Hip-flexor stretch (kneeling lunge):** opens the front of the hips that sitting shortens.
- **Glute bridge:** wakes up the muscles that switch off when you sit.
- **Cat–cow:** gentle spine flexion/extension to free a stiff back.
- **Thoracic rotations (open-book):** restores upper-back rotation for shoulder health.
- **Doorway chest stretch:** counteracts the rounded-shoulder slump.
- **Neck nods + chin tucks:** undo forward-head posture.

Done daily, this beats a once-a-week marathon stretch session.

## Move often, not just once

The best "mobility drill" is simply breaking up sitting. Standing and walking for a minute or two every 30–45 minutes does more than any single routine. Set a reminder, stand during phone calls, walk while revising flashcards.

## Mobility vs flexibility

You don't need to do the splits. **Mobility** is usable range of motion with control — enough to squat properly, reach overhead, and sit tall without strain. That's the goal: pain-free, functional movement, not contortion.

> **Hostel context:** Your hostel room is your mobility studio — the floor, a wall, and a doorway are all you need. Tie the routine to an existing habit: do it right after you wake up, or while a video buffers, or before bed. Two minutes of hip-flexor and chest stretches after a long study session will save your lower back during squats and your shoulders during push-ups.

## Quick FAQ

**Should I stretch before or after workouts?** Do gentle dynamic mobility (leg swings, arm circles, the open-book) before; save longer static holds for after or separate sessions.

**My back aches from sitting — is stretching enough?** Mobility helps, but the bigger fix is *moving more often* and strengthening your glutes and core. Persistent or sharp pain is worth a doctor's look.

**How long until I notice a difference?** Most people feel looser within a week or two of daily practice — it's one of the fastest-rewarding habits.

## Action items

1. Do the 5–8 minute routine daily, anchored to an existing habit.
2. Stand or walk for 1–2 minutes every 30–45 minutes of sitting.
3. Prioritise hips (hip-flexor stretch + glute bridge) and upper back (open-book).
4. Aim for pain-free functional range, not extreme flexibility.
5. Do a quick hip + chest stretch before lower-body or pushing workouts.
