You've heard "1 gram per pound" shouted in every gym reel, but your mess serves dal, rice, and a rotating sabzi — so what are you actually supposed to do? Let's cut the noise and figure out how much protein you need and how to get it without a supplement budget.

## How much protein do you actually need?

For most students who train, the useful range is **1.6 to 2.0 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day**. That's the band where research on muscle gain and retention consistently lands; going much higher rarely adds benefit. So a 60 kg student is aiming for roughly 95–120 g per day. If you're cutting (losing fat), stay near the top of that range to protect muscle; if you're a beginner just getting started, even 1.6 g/kg is plenty.

Notice the unit: **grams per kg of *you*, not a fixed plate size.** A 50 kg person and an 80 kg person have very different targets, which is exactly why generic advice fails.

## Why grams matter more than "eat more protein"

"Eat more protein" is useless because protein is the macro people most consistently *under*-eat, often by half. A katori of dal has only about 7–9 g of protein — satisfying, but you'd need several to hit your number. Once you start counting in grams, you realise a single egg (~6 g), a katori of curd (~3–4 g per 100 g), and a scoop of soya chunks do real work, while one extra spoon of rice does almost nothing for protein.

## Building protein into a mess day

Distribute it across meals rather than chasing it all at dinner — your body uses protein better in 20–30 g doses through the day.

- **Breakfast:** eggs if available, or a glass of milk with your idli/poha.
- **Lunch:** a full katori of dal *plus* the protein sabzi (paneer, chana, rajma, soya); curd on the side.
- **Snack:** roasted chana, peanuts, or another glass of milk.
- **Dinner:** repeat the lunch logic; add eggs or chicken if your mess serves them.

ICMR-NIN's RDA work and standard sports-nutrition guidance both support this "spread it out, count the grams" approach.

> **Hostel context:** Mess protein is thin and inconsistent, so keep two cheap top-ups in your room at all times — a dozen eggs and a packet of roasted chana or a tub of whey if you can afford it. Two boiled eggs after class is a 12 g win that costs less than a samosa. You don't need a fancy diet; you need a reliable floor.

## Common mistakes

- Counting total food, not protein — a big plate of rice is not "a lot of protein."
- Saving all protein for one meal.
- Assuming you need powder. Powder is convenient, not magic; food first.

## Action items

1. Weigh yourself and calculate your target: body weight (kg) × 1.6–2.0.
2. Add up the protein in a typical mess day — you'll likely find a 30–40 g gap.
3. Stock eggs + roasted chana in your room as your daily floor.
4. Put a protein source in *every* meal, not just dinner.
5. Re-check after two weeks; adjust portions, not your whole diet.
