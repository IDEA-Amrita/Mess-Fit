You're "eating a lot," you feel full, the mess plate looks huge — and the scale hasn't budged in three weeks. Frustrating, but it's almost always one of a handful of fixable reasons. None of them are "bad genetics."

## 1. You're eating less than you think

This is the number-one cause, full stop. Appetite is a terrible calorie counter. A plate that *feels* big can still land below your TDEE, especially if it's bulky, watery sabzi and veg. If the scale isn't moving over two weeks, you are — by definition — not in a surplus yet.

**Fix:** add ~250–300 calories of *dense* food, not volume. An extra katori of rice, a glass of milk, a handful of peanuts, or two eggs. Re-check in a week.

## 2. You only "eat big" on some days

Bulking is a weekly average. If you crush it Monday–Thursday and barely eat on a busy weekend, the average can land at maintenance. Your body doesn't reset on Sunday.

**Fix:** keep intake consistent every day, including exam and travel days. Pre-stock room snacks for the chaotic ones.

## 3. Your "surplus" is mostly water and fibre

Filling foods (salad, gourd sabzi, soups) are great for satiety but light on calories — the opposite of what you want when you're struggling to gain.

**Fix:** lean on calorie-dense additions: rice, roti, dairy, nuts, oats, banana. Liquid calories (milk, smoothies) are easy to add when solid food fills you up.

## 4. You're not actually training to grow

A surplus with no progressive training becomes fat, and sometimes the scale doesn't move because you're nervous-eating around junk while under-training. Muscle gain needs a reason to happen.

**Fix:** lift with progressive overload — add reps or load over weeks. Track your sets so you know you're progressing.

## 5. You're weighing yourself wrong

Daily weight swings 1–2 kg from water, salt, and food in your gut. If you weigh at random times you'll see noise, not signal, and conclude "nothing's working."

**Fix:** weigh first thing in the morning, after the toilet, 2–3×/week, and watch the weekly average.

> **Hostel context:** The mess closes, but your gains don't have to. The single highest-leverage move for a hostel hard-gainer is **liquid calories from your room** — one or two glasses of milk a day, or a banana-peanut-butter shake, adds 300–600 easy calories without needing more mess access. Keep milk, oats, peanuts, and bananas stocked.

## Quick FAQ

**How fast should I gain?** About 0.25–0.5 kg per week. Faster is mostly fat, not muscle.

**Should I "dirty bulk" on junk to gain faster?** No — junk adds fat and wrecks energy. You can hit a surplus with milk, nuts, rice, and eggs and feel far better.

**I feel too full to eat more — help?** Use liquid calories (milk, shakes) and calorie-dense foods; they add calories without the fullness of bulky, watery meals.

## Action items

1. Confirm the scale is flat over a true two-week average before changing anything.
2. Add ~300 dense calories/day (milk, nuts, extra rice) and reassess in a week.
3. Eat consistently every day — no "off" days.
4. Make sure your training adds reps or weight over time.
5. Standardise your weigh-ins: morning, post-toilet, same conditions.
