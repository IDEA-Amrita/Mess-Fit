Before you spend money on a flashy pre-workout tub, know this: its main active ingredient is almost always caffeine — and you already have cheaper sources in your hostel. Here's the honest picture on caffeine for training.

## Does caffeine actually help?

Yes — it's one of the few performance aids with strong, repeated evidence. Caffeine can modestly improve strength, endurance, and perceived effort (workouts *feel* easier), and it sharpens focus. For training, it's genuinely useful, not just hype.

## How much, and when

- **Dose:** research clusters around **3–6 mg per kg** of body weight for performance, but most people don't need the high end. A 60 kg student might feel plenty from ~150–200 mg. Start low.
- **Timing:** caffeine peaks roughly **30–60 minutes** after you drink it, so have it before training, not mid-session.
- **Sources:** a cup of coffee has ~80–100 mg; strong chai less; a pre-workout scoop often 150–300 mg. They're interchangeable — your chai or coffee *is* a pre-workout.

## When it backfires

More is not better. Overdoing caffeine causes jitters, a racing heart, anxiety, and stomach upset — none of which help you train. And the big one for students:

- **Sleep.** Caffeine lingers for many hours, so an evening dose can wreck the sleep that actually builds your gains. If you train late, you may be better off skipping caffeine than sabotaging your night.

Tolerance also builds: daily high doses lose their kick, and cutting back briefly restores sensitivity.

## Who should be cautious

If you have anxiety, heart palpitations, acid reflux, or you're sensitive to stimulants, keep the dose low or skip it — and if you have a medical condition or take medication, check with a doctor rather than self-experimenting with high doses.

> **Hostel context:** You don't need an imported pre-workout. A cup of mess/canteen coffee or strong chai ~45 minutes before training does the same job for a fraction of the price. The trap is the *evening* workout: that pre-workout coffee at 8 p.m. can keep you up past midnight, and lost sleep costs you more than the caffeine gains you. Train earlier, or train without it.

## Quick FAQ

**Do I need caffeine to have a good workout?** No. It's a small edge, not a requirement. Plenty of great sessions happen without it.

**Is pre-workout powder worth it over coffee?** Rarely. Most of the effect is caffeine; the extra ingredients are often under-dosed or unproven. Coffee or chai is cheaper and works.

**Why do I feel nothing anymore?** Tolerance. A short break (a week or two at lower intake) restores sensitivity, so you don't need to keep escalating the dose.

**Should I take it on rest days too?** There's no training reason to; if anything, rest days are a good time to let tolerance reset and to protect your sleep.

## Action items

1. Use coffee or chai as your pre-workout — same active ingredient, far cheaper.
2. Take it ~30–60 minutes before training; start with a low dose.
3. Avoid caffeine within ~6–8 hours of bedtime to protect sleep.
4. Don't escalate the dose — jitters mean you've overshot.
5. Be cautious (or skip) if you have anxiety, palpitations, or a relevant condition.
