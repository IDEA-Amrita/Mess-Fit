Protein powder ads make it seem like hitting your protein needs is expensive. It isn't. Some of the best value protein on earth is sitting in your mess and the shop downstairs. Here's how to get your 100-odd grams a day without wrecking your budget.

## The value champions

Ranked roughly by protein-per-rupee and convenience:

- **Eggs** — about 6 g each, cheap, and boilable in a hostel kettle. The default student protein.
- **Milk & curd** — ~3–3.5 g protein per 100 ml/g, plus calcium. A glass of milk is ~8 g.
- **Soya chunks** — the dark horse: roughly 50 g protein per 100 g dry. Cheap, shelf-stable, and they soak up any curry's flavour.
- **Dal & legumes** — dal, rajma, chana, lobia, moong: ~7–9 g per cooked katori, and already in your mess daily.
- **Roasted chana / peanuts** — great room snacks; a handful adds 7–12 g.
- **Paneer** — ~18 g per 100 g; pricier but tasty and filling.
- **Chicken** — excellent quality if your mess or a nearby place serves it.

IFCT 2017 food-composition data backs these ballpark numbers; you don't need to memorise them, just remember eggs, dairy, and legumes are your base.

## Combining for quality

Plant proteins are individually "incomplete," but you don't need to engineer perfect combos at every meal. Eating a **cereal + legume** pairing — rajma-rice, dal-roti, idli-sambar — across the day covers the full amino-acid profile. Variety does the work.

## A cheap 120 g day, roughly

- 3 eggs (18 g) + glass of milk (8 g) at breakfast
- Dal katori (8 g) + paneer/soya sabzi (15 g) + curd (6 g) at lunch
- Roasted chana snack (10 g)
- Dal (8 g) + eggs or chicken (15–25 g) at dinner
- Bedtime glass of milk (8 g)

That's ~100–120 g with minimal extra spend beyond eggs, milk, and chana you keep in your room.

> **Hostel context:** Soya chunks are the single most underrated item for a tight budget — a ₹40 packet can carry a week's worth of supplementary protein. Toss a handful into the mess dal or sabzi (ask, or rehydrate in hot water in your room and mix in). Whey is fine if you can afford it, but don't let "I can't afford supplements" become an excuse — your protein floor is built from eggs, dairy, dal, and soya.

## Quick FAQ

**Is plant protein "worse" than whey?** Per gram it's slightly less concentrated and a single source is less complete, but across a varied day it's perfectly good. Eat enough total and mix sources.

**How many eggs a day is safe?** For healthy people, a few eggs daily is fine for most; if you have a medical condition, ask your doctor.

**Soya and hormones — should men worry?** Normal food amounts of soya don't harm men's hormones; it's a cheap, high-quality protein.

## Action items

1. Set your daily protein target (body weight kg × 1.6–2.0).
2. Stock the room trio: eggs, a tub of roasted chana/peanuts, and soya chunks.
3. Add a cereal+legume pairing at each main meal.
4. Use milk/curd as the easy 8 g top-ups morning and night.
5. Only buy whey *after* food sources still leave a gap.
