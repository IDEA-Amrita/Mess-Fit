You can eat "clean," train hard, and still go nowhere — because the thing that actually decides your weight isn't how healthy your food looks, it's how many calories you eat versus how many you burn. That burn number has a name: TDEE.

## What TDEE means

**TDEE = Total Daily Energy Expenditure** — every calorie your body uses in a day. It has a few parts:

- **BMR (basal metabolic rate):** what you'd burn lying in bed all day. It's the biggest chunk, usually 60–70%.
- **Activity:** walking to class, fidgeting, and your workouts.
- **Digestion:** a small amount burned processing food (protein costs the most to digest).

Eat below your TDEE and you lose weight; above it, you gain; right at it, you maintain. That's the whole game.

## Estimating yours

A widely used, reliable starting point is the **Mifflin-St Jeor equation** for BMR:

`BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + (5 if male, −161 if female)`

Then multiply BMR by an activity factor — roughly 1.2 (sedentary), 1.375 (light, 1–3 sessions/week), 1.55 (moderate, 3–5), up to 1.725 (very active). That product is your TDEE estimate.

Example: a 20-year-old, 60 kg, 175 cm male training 3–4×/week → BMR ≈ 1,605, TDEE ≈ 1,605 × 1.55 ≈ **2,490 kcal/day**.

This is an *estimate*. Treat it as a starting line, not gospel.

## Turning TDEE into a goal

- **Lean bulk:** TDEE + ~250–500 kcal → ~0.25–0.5 kg gain/week.
- **Cut:** TDEE − ~300–500 kcal → ~0.25–0.5 kg loss/week.
- **Maintain:** eat around TDEE.

A safe cap is about ±500 kcal/day; bigger swings just add fat or burn muscle. (This is exactly the math MessFit's goal engine runs to set your daily targets.)

> **Hostel context:** You can't weigh your mess food precisely, and that's fine — your bathroom scale is the real feedback loop. Pick your TDEE estimate, eat to it for two weeks, and watch the *trend*. Gaining when you wanted to cut? Drop a katori of rice or the evening fried snack. The number on the scale over weeks beats any calculator.

## Why the estimate drifts

As you lose weight, your BMR falls (less body to maintain), so your TDEE drifts down — a cut that worked at first can stall. The fix isn't panic; it's a small further adjustment. Likewise, as you gain, TDEE rises. Recalculate every few kg.

## Quick FAQ

**Do I need to count calories forever?** No. Use it to learn portion sizes for a few weeks; most people then eyeball it and rely on the scale trend.

**My friend eats more and stays lean — unfair?** They likely move more (higher activity factor) or eat less than you think on average. TDEE is individual; compare yourself to your own trend, not to them.

**Should I eat back exercise calories?** Your activity factor already includes training, so don't double-count by adding workout calories on top.

## Action items

1. Calculate your BMR with Mifflin-St Jeor, then multiply by your activity factor.
2. Pick a goal and set your intake: TDEE ±300–500 kcal.
3. Weigh yourself 2–3×/week, same conditions, and track the weekly average.
4. After 2 weeks, compare actual change to your goal and nudge intake by ~200 kcal if needed.
5. Recalculate TDEE after every ~3–4 kg of weight change.
